On Language, Thought & the Word

"The Word" Is Not the Word

Every language works this way. You translate something, and you lose a little of it in the crossing.

But sometimes what gets lost isn't small.

When I found out that the Greek word behind "the Word" in John's Gospel is logos, I was surprised at how much narrower our English word actually is. Word, to us, means something spoken. A sound. A sentence. Logos meant something closer to reason itself - the ordering principle behind how things hold together.

Sit with that for a second.

If Jesus is the logos, it isn't just that God spoke and things appeared. It's that God spoke, and then used Jesus to build the actual architecture the universe runs on. The precision underneath everything. The reason water freezes exactly where it needs to, the reason gravity holds at exactly the right measure for anything to exist at all.

Calling that "the Word" almost minimizes it.

John 1:1 says, "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." Colossians 1:16-17 says "in him all things were created" and "in him all things hold together."

Read those with logos in mind instead of word, and they stop sounding like poetry. They start sounding like architecture.

I don't think this changes what we believe. I think it just asks us to believe it more precisely.

Maybe that's true of most things worth understanding. The first definition is rarely the whole one. You have to dig a little deeper to find what was actually being said.
