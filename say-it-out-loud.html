On Language, Thought & the Word

Say It Out Loud

Have you ever been stuck on something, tried to explain it to someone, and halfway through thought, "Oh. I know what I need to do."

That's happened to me more times than I can count.

Years ago, when I worked as a Salesforce administrator, sometimes the best thing I could do with a problem was stop staring at the screen and go grab coffee with a friend. I'd start explaining what wasn't working, and suddenly the answer would become obvious.

My friend didn't solve the problem. Half the time, he barely had to say anything.

I just needed to say it out loud.

There's something about putting our thoughts into words that forces us to slow down and make sense of them. Things can feel perfectly reasonable - or completely overwhelming - when they're bouncing around inside our heads. Then we try to explain them, and suddenly we hear them differently.

I see it with my kids, too. Sometimes they struggle to say what they're thinking or feeling. But if I'm being fair, adults aren't much better.

We worry we'll sound foolish. We don't want to be judged. Sometimes we don't even know what we're feeling ourselves.

So we keep it inside.

That's why having someone safe to talk to matters. Not necessarily because they have the answers, but because sometimes we discover something simply by being heard.

And yet, there are parts of us that are harder to understand.

Jeremiah 17:9 says, "The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it?"

That's humbling because sometimes the person I have the hardest time understanding honestly is myself.

Am I making a good decision - or just convincing myself that I am?

Jesus gave a surprisingly practical test: look at the fruit.

What is this choice producing in my life? Is it making me more loving, patient, honest and peaceful? Is it helping or hurting the people around me?

And that's also why prayer matters so much to me.

Talking to another person can help me organize my thoughts. But prayer reminds me that I don't have to have them perfectly organized in the first place.

Jesus said in Matthew 6:8 that God knows what we need before we even ask.

Sometimes I need a friend who will listen.

Sometimes I need to hear my own thoughts out loud.

And sometimes I need to stop talking long enough to listen.

Maybe the most important prayer isn't complicated at all:

"Not my will, but Yours be done."
