On Language, Thought & the Word

Too Familiar to See

What a 19th-century doctor and a rejected church founder have in common.

A doctor had the data. The death rate dropped dramatically when he introduced handwashing. The medical establishment rejected him anyway.

His name was Ignaz Semmelweis. In the 1840s, he discovered that physicians in Vienna were killing their patients by going straight from performing autopsies to delivering babies - without washing their hands. He introduced a chlorinated handwashing solution and the mortality rate collapsed.

It didn't matter. Germ theory - the idea that specific microorganisms cause specific diseases - didn't exist yet. Without that framework, his explanation didn't fit what doctors already believed. He lost his position. He was ridiculed. He died before the scientific community admitted he was right.

The truth didn't change. Their ability to receive it was the variable.

1 Corinthians 1:27 says, "But God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise."

I grew up Catholic. Over the years my parents took me to a few different churches. At one of them, I stood up publicly and said I wanted Jesus in my life. I wanted him to save me. People prayed over me.

And then we left. Nobody ever said another word about it.

Not because they didn't care. I think they genuinely didn't know what came next. In that framework, I was saved. Done. But James 2:26 says faith without deeds is dead. And if there's nothing left to do, I'm not sure what you're left with.

When I eventually found a church that felt true, I joined. The founder had been asked to recant her position on the Trinity by people with money and influence on their side. She said no. She walked away from what that money could have built because she wasn't willing to trade the truth for it.

Isaiah 53:3 says, "He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows, and familiar with suffering."

When I told people in my life I'd joined, they told me I'd joined a cult.

That's the pattern. Something true shows up and it doesn't look the way we expected. It doesn't come from the places we were told to look. So we reject it, or mock it, or wait until enough time passes that it becomes safe to accept.

Semmelweis was right about handwashing twenty years before anyone would say so out loud.

John 1:11 says, "He came to that which was his own, but his own did not receive him."

What truth might you be dismissing right now simply because it doesn't fit the framework you already have?
